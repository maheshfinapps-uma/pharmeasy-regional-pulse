# PharmaEasy Regional Pulse — Presentation Storyline

## 1. Executive Audience — Situation–Complication–Resolution (SCR)

### Situation

Guntur regional sales increased from ₹62,442.27 in April 2026 to ₹138,738.93 in May 2026, representing a **+122.19% month-over-month movement**. The April–June analysis uses cleaned order data and an 8% operational alert threshold.

### Complication

The +122.19% April-to-May movement is substantially above the 8% operational alert threshold and was followed by a decline to ₹99,745.18 in June, representing a **-28.11% movement**. The data confirms a sharp increase followed by a partial reversal, but the regional aggregate alone does not establish the underlying cause.

### Resolution

Review Guntur’s order-level and category-level details for April, May, and June before taking operational action. The next step is to determine whether the movement was broad-based or concentrated in particular orders or categories, using the dashboard’s category breakdown and regional/monthly detail.

## 2. Regional Manager Audience — Overview–Category–Detail (OCD)

### Overview

Guntur sales increased by **+122.19%** from April to May 2026, rising from ₹62,442.27 to ₹138,738.93. This movement was flagged by the 8% operational alert rule.

### Category

The current regional aggregate verifies that Guntur experienced the increase, but it does not by itself verify which medicine category caused the movement. The category-level dashboard view should be used to compare Guntur’s sales contribution across the six categories for April and May and establish whether the change was concentrated or broad-based.

### Detail

The supporting monthly sales evidence is:

- April 2026: ₹62,442.27
- May 2026: ₹138,738.93
- June 2026: ₹99,745.18
- April-to-May movement: +122.19%
- May-to-June movement: -28.11%

These figures were calculated from cleaned order data using regional-month sales aggregation. The 8% threshold is an operational alert rule, not a statistical significance test. Order-level and category-level review is required before attributing a cause to the movement.

## 3. Anticipated Stakeholder Pushback Q&A

### Q1. Why should I believe this number?

**1. Acknowledge the concern**

It is reasonable to question whether a +122.19% movement is caused by a data-quality issue, duplicate records, or an aggregation error.

**2. Verified vs. not verified**

- **Verified:** Guntur sales were ₹62,442.27 in April and ₹138,738.93 in May.
- **Verified:** The movement is +122.19% and exceeds the 8% operational alert threshold.
- **Verified:** The calculation uses cleaned order data and regional-month aggregation.
- **Not yet verified:** Whether the increase is caused by a specific order, category, business event, or data-quality issue.

**3. Resolution and timing**

Reconcile Guntur’s April and May order-level records, check duplicate/order-count consistency, and compare category contributions. This review should be completed **before the next operational decision or stakeholder review**, with the dashboard detail view used to document the result.

---

### Q2. What if an alternative explanation is driving this?

**1. Acknowledge the concern**

An alternative explanation may be driving the movement, and the regional aggregate alone cannot distinguish among demand changes, one-off orders, category concentration, or other operational factors.

**2. Verified vs. not verified**

- **Verified:** Sales increased by +122.19% from April to May.
- **Verified:** Sales then declined by -28.11% from May to June.
- **Not yet verified:** The role of promotions, seasonality, one-off transactions, stock availability, customer mix, or other external operational factors.

**3. Resolution and timing**

Compare order-level and category-level contributions for April, May, and June, then review any available business context with the regional team. Complete this cause-validation review **before assigning an operational explanation or taking corrective action**.

---

### Q3. What would change your recommendation?

**1. Acknowledge the concern**

The recommendation to investigate before acting should change if the detailed evidence shows that the movement is either a repeatable demand pattern or a data-quality problem.

**2. Verified vs. not verified**

- **Verified:** The current evidence supports investigation of the sharp increase and subsequent reversal.
- **Not yet verified:** Whether the movement is broad-based, repeatable, concentrated in one category, or caused by a data issue.

**3. Resolution and timing**

If order- and category-level evidence shows a broad-based, repeatable pattern, the team can move from investigation to monitoring and operational planning. If it shows a duplicate, correction, or one-off transaction, the dashboard and supporting records should be annotated or corrected. Make this determination **after the next order- and category-level review**.

---

### Q4. What did you not check?

**1. Acknowledge the concern**

It is important to distinguish what the dashboard verifies from what remains outside the current analysis.

**2. Verified vs. not verified**

- **Verified:** Regional monthly sales, month-over-month movements, distinct order counts, category sales breakdowns, and the operational alert rule are included.
- **Not yet verified:** The business cause of the Guntur movement, promotion history, stock availability, customer-level behavior, and any external market factors.

**3. Resolution and timing**

Add the relevant operational context and, where available, promotion, inventory, and customer-mix information to the next review. Complete these additional checks **before presenting a causal explanation to executives**.
